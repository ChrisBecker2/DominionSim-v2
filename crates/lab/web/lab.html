Challengers

After the ranking, each round evolves a best response to the current Nash mix. If it beats the mix by more than the margin it joins the table (3 = above 53%); the first round that fails ends the loop. 0 rounds = off.

Scoring weights

A candidate's score is its win rate against a weighted mix of the baselines, the newest champions and some older ones (weights are shares; empty groups drop out).

The most rules a strategy may have.

Exploiter rounds

Final ranking

Crosstable: the row strategy beats the column strategy this often (ties count half; hover for the 95% interval). Mean = average over the other strategies. Nash = share of an approximate equilibrium mix of the strategies: only strategies in the mix are not beaten by a combination of the others. Exploitability: how much the strongest challenger found beats this strategy (win rate minus 50%, at least 0). Near 0 = hard to beat. If several strategies share the Nash mix, they beat each other in a cycle.

Findings: what decides games on this kingdom

At the end of a run the champion plays every other strategy of the ranking, the baselines included (what decides its games, what each card is worth, every opening it played), and each of its rules is taken out in turn: what each is worth is written as a comment above it in the result's file. Fast bots. Points are win-rate percentage points.

Compare two strategies
Plays two strategies against each other and shows what happens: opening splits, what is gained and trashed on each turn, the win rate by copies of a card, decision trees over the position, the gains that turn games, and (optional) what each rule of strategy A is worth.
